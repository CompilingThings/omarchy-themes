# Cold Blood

An Omarchy theme of frogs, lizards, snakes and crocodiles photographed so close that every scale and the pattern
inside each eye shows: a red-eyed tree frog, two chameleons, three pit vipers, a green-eyed gecko, a crocodile's
armoured head. The black of a night photo, pale green-white text, the red of the tree frog's eye as the accent, and
the window border fading from that red into the green of Gumprecht's pit viper. The terminal colours come from the
photos too: the leaf under the tree frog, the yellow eyelash viper, chameleon turquoise, the rainbow sheen on a
python's scales.

(All of them are what people call cold-blooded: animals that rely on outside heat, not their own, to keep warm
([Wikipedia: Ectotherm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectotherm)).)

Each wallpaper has a small label in the bottom right corner (bottom right because terminal text keeps to the
left): the animal, one line in plain words, when it was photographed, the photographer and the licence.
Six photos with bright colour or busy scales under the label (01, 02, 04, 06, 10, 12) get a dark panel behind it.

Facts on the labels come from each photo's Commons page (name, place, date, description) and Wikipedia: the
red-eyed tree frog's eyes have "a nictitating membrane that allows light to enter the eye even when closed"
([Agalychnis callidryas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agalychnis_callidryas)); a chameleon's "upper and lower
eyelids are joined, with only a pinhole large enough for the pupil", and "each eye can pivot and focus
independently" ([Chameleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon)); *Gekko smithii* "is one of the biggest
geckos", up to 35 cm with its tail ([Gekko smithii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gekko_smithii)); the reticulated
python's "mainland subspecies is the world's longest snake"
([Reticulated python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reticulated_python)); the dwarf crocodile is "the smallest extant
living species of crocodile" with "a heavily armoured neck, back, and tail"
([Dwarf crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_crocodile)); *Rhamnophis aethiopissa* "preys upon frogs,
lizards, birds, and small mammals" ([Large-eyed green tree snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-eyed_green_tree_snake));
the crawfish frog "inhabits the burrows of crayfish for most of the year"
([Crawfish frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawfish_frog)); the American crocodile "is the only species other
than the saltwater crocodile to commonly live and thrive in saltwater"
([American crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_crocodile)); Bengal monitors "have a forked tongue
similar to snakes", mainly for sensing ([Bengal monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_monitor)). The date
is the one the photo file records.

Chosen from Wikimedia Commons' featured pictures of frogs, salamanders, lizards, snakes and crocodiles and searches
for gecko, chameleon, frog, snake and lizard eyes and the red-eyed tree frog (about 370 landscape photos at least
3000 pixels wide), keeping close-ups only: a head, an eye or skin filling the frame. Every photo was uploaded by its
photographer or imported from Flickr with the licence checked (FlickreviewR or Flickypedia); none is
non-commercial or no-changes. Grain at screen size (`src/grain.py`, Immerkaer sigma at 2560 x 1440): eyelash viper
0.3, pit viper 0.4, bushfrog 0.6, python 0.6, crawfish frog 0.6, Indian chameleon 0.9, tree frog 0.9, gecko 1.5,
water frog 1.8, dwarf crocodile 2.3, tree snake 2.7, monitor 3.9, American crocodile 4.1, chameleon eye 4.3. The
water frog and everything from 2.3 up were looked at in true-size crops: the high numbers are real scale texture, not
grain. Left out: a striking leaf-tailed gecko eye (an old scan with halos and speckle at full size) and a second
green pit viper (too close to 03).

```
src/build cold-blood             # the photos (~110 MB) download once into ~/.cache/omarchy-themes/cold-blood
./install cold-blood
omarchy theme set cold-blood
```
